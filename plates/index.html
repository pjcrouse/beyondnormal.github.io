This is the whole tool

One number in, one loadout back.

Type the weight. It draws the bar — biggest plate to smallest, in the order you'd actually slide them on — and lists the per-side breakdown underneath.

Ask for a weight the plates can't build and it tells you the closest one you can, instead of quietly rounding and letting you find out at the rack.

345 lb on a 45 lb bar → 150 per side: 3 × 45, 1 × 10, 1 × 5
What it does

Built for the rack, not a spreadsheet.

No sign-in, no setup, no ads between you and the answer. Open it, type a number, load the bar.

Per-side, drawn out

Not a wall of text — the actual stack, biggest plate to smallest, in the order you'd slide them on. Glance and load.

Your bar, not a default

45 lb, 35 lb, a 20 kg women's bar, a 25 lb fixed bar for curls. Set the bar and the math follows it.

Kilos are native

A kg lifter loads a 20 kg bar with 25s, 20s, 15s and 1.25s — real kilo plates, not pounds with a converted label stuck on.

The inventory

What it assumes is on the rack.

Pounds

Standard commercial-gym set:

  • 45, 35, 25, 10, 5, 2.5 lb plates
  • Bars from 45 lb down to fixed and specialty bars
  • Rounds to the nearest 5 lb — the smallest change you can make with a pair of 2.5s

Kilograms

Standard metric set:

  • 25, 20, 15, 10, 5, 2.5, 1.25 kg plates
  • 20 kg and 15 kg bars
  • Rounds to the nearest 2.5 kg — a pair of 1.25s
